Competitive-Shooting Video Editor for iPhone, iPad, and Mac

Shooting Cut is a complete competitive-shooting video editor for iPhone, iPad, and Mac: trim one stage, synchronize POV and third-person footage, combine every stage, add verified timing and score context, reframe for vertical formats, and export from one product.

Updated: 2026-07-30

The editing job is usually simple to describe even when the footage is not: remove dead time, keep simultaneous camera views aligned, put sequential stages in order, and make the finished run understandable. Shooting Cut organizes those jobs around match footage rather than presenting timer and gunshot subtitles as the entire product.

Outcome map

Finish the common match-video jobs in one editor

Shooting Cut lets you finish the common match-video jobs in one editor, then use the workflow whose inputs match the footage:

  • One stage recording: remove dead time before and after the run while keeping the proposed markers reviewable.
  • POV plus third-person footage: align simultaneous camera views of the same run by timer-beep evidence.
  • A complete match: put sequential stages in competition order and combine up to 20 clips.
  • Timing and score context: make performance understandable with the verified fields available in an imported result.
  • More than one publishing shape: reuse one edit across supported publishing formats after reviewing each composition.
  • Several prepared outputs: render separate match edits with macOS Pro batch export instead of confusing batch output with one merged timeline.

Sequential and simultaneous are different. Merge is for clips that should play one after another. Split Sync and Stage Mix are for simultaneous views of the same run. macOS batch export processes separate output jobs; it is not the same as joining stages into one full-match video.

One clip or every stage

Edit one stage or assemble a complete match

For one recording, Auto Trim analyzes the timer beep and gunshot events, identifies the active stage segment, and removes unused footage before and after the run. Review the detected timer and shot markers before export.

For a complete match, review the finished stage clips, place Stage 1 through Stage N in competition order, and use Merge to combine up to 20 clips into one long video. That workflow fits the full-match videos many shooters publish on YouTube or keep as a personal competition archive.

Simultaneous views

Synchronize two views or direct a multi-camera stage

Split Sync takes exactly 2 simultaneous recordings and aligns them by the timer beep. Choose Full Screen, Dual Center HUD, Dual Top HUD, or Side by Side according to how much context and performance data the finished video should show.

Stage Mix takes 2–3 simultaneous inputs labeled POV, Follow, or Static. Its movement-aware switching proposes camera changes during the run, and manual override lets you replace any cut. Use it when a stage needs more than a fixed two-view composition.

Editing signal

Timer and shot analysis supports the edit

Shooting Cut listens for timer beeps and gunshot impulses to help locate a run, synchronize simultaneous recordings, calculate timing information, and place performance data. The analysis is one editing capability among trimming, merging, multi-camera composition, scoring, tracking, and export.

Recording conditions still matter. Automatic gain control (AGC), neighboring gunshots, echo or reverb, and a weak timer beep can affect detection. Check the timer marker, individual shots, last-shot marker, sensitivity, and minimum split before final export. For additional help, use the support page or the FAQ.

Results and timing

Add score-aware overlays

Shooting Cut can import results from supported PractiScore, ESS, and HDP/IDPA sources. Imported records can associate match, stage, shooter, and score information with the related video.

When PractiScore provides an official per-shot timing record, Shooting Cut can anchor those official shot times to the footage using the timer beep and use the available times, splits, shot count, and score data in video subtitles. ESS and HDP/IDPA support score import, but they do not provide the same PractiScore-style official per-shot anchoring source.

One edit, more formats

Reframe one edit for more than one publishing format

Reframe/Track uses on-device person tracking to keep a selected shooter inside a supported portrait or square crop. You can return to the same edit and export another supported format for a different publishing workflow instead of recording the stage again.

Available ratios and resolutions depend on the source, editing mode, and export ceiling. A tracked crop is not automatically available in every ratio-and-resolution combination. You can save an export for any destination. Direct upload to YouTube and Facebook is a Pro feature. It requires separate platform authorization.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

What is the best way to edit a full shooting match?

Prepare each stage clip, put Stage 1 through Stage N in competition order, and use Merge to combine up to 20 sequential clips into one long full-match video. Use Split Sync or Stage Mix instead when the inputs are simultaneous views of the same stage.

Which Shooting Cut mode should I use?

Use Auto Trim for exactly 1 video, Merge for up to 20 sequential clips, Split Sync for exactly 2 simultaneous views, or Stage Mix for 2–3 simultaneous inputs labeled POV, Follow, or Static.

Is gunshot detection the main Shooting Cut feature?

Gunshot and timer analysis supports trimming, synchronization, timing data, and overlays, but Shooting Cut is a broader editor with sequential match assembly, multi-camera workflows, score-aware overlays, subject tracking, reframing, and export.

What is free, and what does Pro add?

Auto Trim and score import are free. Free Auto Trim exports include the Shooting Cut watermark and logo intro card. Pro removes the watermark, enables custom intro cards, unlocks Split Sync and Stage Mix, and enables macOS batch export. One subscription covers all your Apple devices.
